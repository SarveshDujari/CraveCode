# Phase 2 status

The meal-brief form now has an optional dish field. If it is empty, a bounded planner chooses up to four themes from the diet and goal. A configured Gemma endpoint can suggest alternative queries, with schema validation, diet guardrails, and a deterministic fallback. The signed-in local path searches multiple Swiggy menu queries, deduplicates by restaurant/menu ID, excludes unavailable items and unconfirmed vegetarian items for vegetarian requests, respects simple exclusions such as “no mushrooms,” and ranks the remaining menu items with visible reasons and cautions. Search requests run through a Mastra plan → discover → verify workflow. Sentry stage spans are available when `SENTRY_DSN` is configured.

The public Render form asks for a city instead of showing a disabled Swiggy address dropdown. With `SERPAPI_API_KEY`, it searches public food pages through SerpApi and shows linked **web leads**. These are not Swiggy menu items, and no availability, price, dietary classification, macros, or delivery total is claimed. Without the key, the site shows a clear setup state. MongoDB Atlas stores search jobs and candidates when `MONGODB_URI` is configured; saved briefs omit the Swiggy address ID and free-text wish.

The current ranking is useful for discovery but **does not prove the user's protein, calorie, or all-in budget targets**. Exact nutrition-source matching, hosted Gemma verification, public Swiggy OAuth, verified cart totals, and job resume remain open. Public Swiggy OAuth cannot go live until production access is approved and server sessions/tokens are protected and durable. Cart verification remains in Phase 3.

Verification in this workspace: the TypeScript/Vite/server build and 18 tests pass, including goal-based planning, diet/stock/exclusion filtering, deduplication, delivery-site and city filtering, honest web-lead labeling, and both Mastra workflow modes. The owner signed in to Swiggy in their own browser, searched locally with the optional dish field empty, and confirmed ranked live menu cards appear. A Render-mode browser preview shows the city field and optional dish. The deployed Phase 2 endpoint now reports `searchRevision: 3`; one live Bengaluru paneer request returned three relevant Swiggy/Zomato web leads and no warnings. Its earlier response had contained unrelated and other-city pages, which the new filters removed in that sample. This single live search does not establish broad search quality or nutrition coverage. Atlas persistence and hosted Gemma require configuration and are not marked verified.

## Search quality update awaiting live verification

The next revision raises the shortlist limit from 12 to 15 and asks SerpApi for up to 10 organic results per query without increasing the number of Google queries. Local Swiggy search now requests restaurant ratings and review counts, checks OPEN status, and labels limited or missing feedback. A form preference softly boosts well-reviewed restaurants; it never asserts that reviews prove food hygiene. Public web leads keep restaurant ratings unknown because they cannot be matched confidently to the Swiggy restaurant ID. Gemma receives the numeric calorie/protein goals to propose dish themes, while rejecting calorie-claim phrases and never asserting that an item meets the goal.

The previous local-only Gemma calorie guesses were discarded after the owner observed repeated estimates. An on-demand SerpApi reference lookup can show a generic food's calories and protein only when Google returns a comparable description, source link, and serving size. It remains explicitly separate from the restaurant's actual nutrition and cannot make an exact-match claim. All 18 automated checks and the production build pass. This revision still needs a live local Swiggy rating check and a Render deployment check.
